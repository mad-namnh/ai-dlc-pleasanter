## 5. Accepted boundaries

None claimed for WS-3 criteria. ADR-0014 (Proposed) explicitly lists "recovery status after a failed restore" and "handling of a timeout after the server has written" as **gaps, not boundaries** (`docs/adr/0014-trust-boundaries.md:44-48`) — C8 and C10 are therefore FAIL. TB-8 (API limit, no conditional write) is a valid boundary **for the limit itself**, but its compensating control is shown not to hold (WS3-F5), so C9 is PARTIAL, not ACCEPTED BOUNDARY. Foreign exports passed to `mark-applied` by hand fall partly under TB-1 (operator files), but the missing `SiteId` / status check is code-closable (WS3-F3).

## 7. Top risks (impact × likelihood)

1. **WS3-F3 + WS3-F1 — a failed or half-done apply becomes "applied" in git** (High, demo). After a failed restore or a timeout the obvious reconciliation step (`export` + `mark-applied`) records `applied-differs` and asks to fold the old state into `config/`, silently reverting part of the release in the desired state.
2. **WS3-F2 — timeout after the server wrote** (High, cstg; likely on slow on-prem servers). Traceback, no record, no restore; bootstrap re-run duplicates sites.
3. **WS3-F6 / WS3-F7 — sandbox writes reach shared dev data** (High, demo). A script added after `sandbox create` or an extra plan step bypasses the shared-site protection.
4. **WS3-F4 — evidence overwritten** (High). The release folder can claim "planned / refused" for a release whose sites were written.
5. **WS3-F5 — lost update between preflight and write** (Medium). Undetected by design today; TB-8 overstates the control.
